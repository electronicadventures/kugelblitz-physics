Cognitive verbs.

A short reference for the verbs used in question stems. Each verb tells you what kind of answer is wanted — how brief, how detailed, whether to show working or to reason.

Click any verb in a quiz question and you’ll land at its entry below. Definitions are aligned to the QCAA cognitive-verb glossary, adapted for junior science.

Analytical

Analyse

Examine in detail by breaking something into its parts to study or explain it.

You should

  • Identify the key components, features, or factors
  • Show how the parts relate to each other
  • Point out patterns, trends, or relationships

Example prompt

Analyse the relationship between dot spacing and velocity on a ticker tape.

What a good answer looks like

The spacing between dots represents the distance the trolley moved during the 0.02 s between dots, so v = d/t. Larger spacing means a higher velocity at that point. As the tape progresses, the spacings grow, showing the trolley speeds up uniformly — evidence of constant positive acceleration.
Explanatory

Apply

Use knowledge, a method, or a principle to solve a problem in a specific situation.

You should

  • Identify the relevant formula or principle
  • Show how it fits the situation
  • Carry out the calculation or reasoning

Example prompt

Apply the equation v = u + a·t to find the final velocity of a cyclist starting from rest who accelerates at 1.5 m/s² for 4 seconds.

What a good answer looks like

u = 0 m/s, a = 1.5 m/s², t = 4 s. Using v = u + a·t: v = 0 + 1.5 × 4 = 6 m/s.
Mathematical

Calculate

Determine a numerical answer using mathematical operations.

You should

  • State the formula you are using
  • Substitute the values
  • Give the final answer with correct units

Example prompt

Calculate the velocity of a car that travels 100 m in 5 seconds.

What a good answer looks like

v = d / t
v = 100 m / 5 s
v = 20 m/s
Conceptual

Classify

Group items into categories based on shared characteristics.

You should

  • State which category the item belongs to
  • When asked, give a brief reason why

Example prompt

Classify distance as either a scalar or a vector.

What a good answer looks like

Distance is a scalar quantity because it has magnitude (size) but no direction.
Analytical

Compare

Show how things are similar AND different across specific points.

You should

  • Identify points of comparison (e.g. units, direction, sign)
  • Address each point for both items
  • Use linking language: similarly, both, in contrast, however

Example prompt

Compare distance and displacement.

What a good answer looks like

Both distance and displacement are measured in metres and describe how far an object has moved. They differ in that distance is a scalar (magnitude only) while displacement is a vector (magnitude and direction). For an object moving in a straight line, distance and the magnitude of displacement are equal; for a curved or back-and-forth path they are usually different.
Mathematical

Convert

Change a value from one unit of measurement to another.

You should

  • State (or use) the conversion factor
  • Show the arithmetic clearly
  • Give the result with the new unit

Example prompt

Convert 2.5 km to metres.

What a good answer looks like

1 km = 1000 m
2.5 km × 1000 = 2500 m
Conceptual

Define

Give the precise meaning of a word, phrase or concept.

You should

  • Write a concise, accurate statement
  • Use technical or scientific language where appropriate
  • Leave out details that are not part of the definition

Example prompt

Define velocity.

What a good answer looks like

Velocity is the rate of change of position. It is a vector quantity, meaning it has both magnitude (speed) and direction.
Descriptive

Describe

Provide a detailed account of features, characteristics or events.

You should

  • Use clear, structured sentences
  • Include specific details: what, where, when, how
  • Stick to the facts — explanation is not required

Example prompt

Describe the motion shown on a ticker tape with evenly spaced dots.

What a good answer looks like

The dots on the tape are spaced equally from start to end. This means the trolley travelled the same distance during each 0.02 s interval, so the motion is constant velocity (uniform motion in a straight line).
Mathematical

Determine

Establish or work out the answer to a problem, using calculation or reasoning.

You should

  • Identify the most appropriate method
  • Show working where calculation is involved
  • Give a clear final answer

Example prompt

Determine the magnitude of the displacement when a student walks 100 m east then 50 m west.

What a good answer looks like

Taking east as positive: total displacement = +100 m − 50 m = +50 m. The magnitude of the displacement is 50 m (direction: east).
Analytical

Distinguish

Recognise or point out the differences between things.

You should

  • Clearly state how the items differ
  • Use contrasting language: whereas, unlike, however
  • Cover all the key differences

Example prompt

Distinguish between speed and velocity.

What a good answer looks like

Speed is a scalar — it tells you how fast an object is moving but not in which direction. Velocity is a vector — it tells you both the speed AND the direction of motion. A car driving 60 km/h has a speed of 60 km/h, but its velocity is 60 km/h north (or whatever direction it is travelling).
Evaluative

Evaluate

Make a judgement about value, quality, importance or effectiveness, supported by evidence.

You should

  • State your judgement clearly
  • Give evidence that supports it
  • Often weigh pros and cons before concluding

Example prompt

Evaluate the use of a ticker timer for measuring acceleration in a school lab.

What a good answer looks like

Ticker timers are a low-cost and effective way to record motion at 50 Hz, giving students a tangible record of dot spacings to measure and calculate velocity and acceleration from. However, they introduce friction on the tape and small reading errors at each interval, which can compound when calculating acceleration. Overall they are well suited to introductory teaching of kinematics but are less accurate than a digital motion sensor for quantitative experiments.
Explanatory

Explain

Provide details about why or how something happens, using cause and effect.

You should

  • Show reasoning, not just facts
  • Use connecting words: because, therefore, so, as a result
  • Link multiple ideas into a coherent answer

Example prompt

Explain why an object in free fall accelerates downward.

What a good answer looks like

An object in free fall is acted on by the force of gravity, which pulls it toward the centre of the Earth. Because there is no other force balancing gravity (ignoring air resistance), the net force on the object is unbalanced and points downward. According to Newton’s second law (F = ma), this unbalanced force causes the object to accelerate downward at approximately 9.8 m/s².
Brief

Identify

Recognise and name a specific feature, item or quality.

You should

  • Give a brief, direct response
  • Name the specific thing being asked about
  • No explanation is required

Example prompt

Identify the SI unit of acceleration.

What a good answer looks like

m/s² (metres per second squared)
Analytical

Investigate

Carry out a systematic examination or study to discover information.

You should

  • Plan a method (variables, equipment, procedure)
  • Collect and record data
  • Analyse the data and draw a conclusion

Example prompt

Investigate how the mass of a trolley affects its acceleration when pulled by a constant force.

What a good answer looks like

Method: pull a trolley along a track with a constant force (e.g. a hanging mass over a pulley) and use a ticker timer to record motion. Repeat with different trolley masses (e.g. by adding standard masses). Result: as mass increases, acceleration decreases. This supports a = F/m — acceleration is inversely proportional to mass when F is constant.
Explanatory

Justify

Give reasons that support an argument, conclusion or decision.

You should

  • State the position or claim
  • Provide evidence or reasoning
  • Explain why the evidence supports the claim

Example prompt

Justify why displacement is a vector quantity.

What a good answer looks like

Displacement is a vector because it has both magnitude and direction. For example, walking 50 m east is a different displacement from walking 50 m west, even though the magnitudes are the same. The direction is an essential part of the quantity, which is the defining feature of a vector.
Brief

List

Provide a series of items, names, points or features.

You should

  • Be brief and direct — sentences are not required
  • Present items as a list, separated by commas or bullets
  • Give the number of items asked for

Example prompt

List three vector quantities.

What a good answer looks like

velocity, force, displacement
Descriptive

Outline

Give a brief summary of the main features or steps.

You should

  • Cover only the essentials, not the details
  • Use short statements, often numbered or bulleted
  • Keep it structured (steps in order, features grouped)

Example prompt

Outline the steps to calculate acceleration from a ticker tape.

What a good answer looks like

1. Measure the first interval to find v_i (v = d/t with t = 0.02 s).
2. Measure the last interval to find v_f.
3. Count the number of intervals and multiply by 0.02 s to find total time.
4. Use a = (v_f − v_i) / t.
Mathematical

Predict

State what is likely to happen, based on observation, data, or theory.

You should

  • Give a specific, testable statement of what you expect
  • Often include a numerical value
  • Give a brief reason (because…)

Example prompt

Predict the velocity of a trolley after 3 seconds of constant acceleration at 2 m/s², starting from rest.

What a good answer looks like

Using v = u + a·t with u = 0, a = 2 m/s², t = 3 s: v = 0 + 2 × 3 = 6 m/s. The trolley is predicted to be moving at 6 m/s after 3 seconds.
Brief

Recall

Bring something back to mind from memory.

You should

  • Give a brief, factual answer
  • No working or explanation required

Example prompt

Recall the SI unit of force.

What a good answer looks like

Newton (N)
Brief

State

Express something clearly and concisely. Often used for formulas, values or laws.

You should

  • Give a brief, factual response
  • No working or explanation required
  • Use correct technical notation (units, subscripts, etc.)

Example prompt

State the formula for acceleration.

What a good answer looks like

a = (v_f − v_i) / t
