## Four spheres, one system

<p>Scientists split Earth into four connected parts called <strong>spheres</strong>:</p><ul><li><strong>Atmosphere:</strong> the layer of air around Earth.</li><li><strong>Hydrosphere:</strong> all the water: oceans, rivers, ice and water vapour.</li><li><strong>Biosphere:</strong> all living things.</li><li><strong>Geosphere:</strong> rocks, soil and Earth's interior, including <strong>fossil fuels</strong> (coal, oil and gas).</li></ul><p>Energy and matter keep moving between the spheres. Carbon is a good example. A tree takes CO<sub>2</sub> from the air (atmosphere → biosphere). The ocean dissolves CO<sub>2</sub> (atmosphere → hydrosphere). Plants buried for millions of years become coal (biosphere → geosphere). Burning that coal sends the carbon straight back into the air in a few seconds (geosphere → atmosphere).</p><p><img src="../../img/y10c01-spheres-model.png" alt="Model of Earth&#x27;s four spheres. Sunlight passes through the atmosphere and warms the surface; the surface gives off infrared, some escapes to space and some is absorbed by greenhouse gases and sent back down. Carbon arrows show photosynthesis, respiration and decay, burning fossil fuels, the ocean dissolving and releasing CO2, shells forming limestone, buried plants forming coal, and volcanoes" width="680" style="max-width:100%;" loading="lazy"></p><div class="admonition tip"><p style='margin:0;'><strong>Key idea:</strong> carbon moves slowly into the geosphere (millions of years) but burning fossil fuels moves it back out <em>very fast</em>. That is the imbalance behind today's warming.</p></div>

## The greenhouse effect: natural, then enhanced

<p>Sunlight is mostly visible light. It passes through the air and warms the ground and ocean. The warm surface then gives off energy as <strong>infrared</strong> radiation (heat you cannot see).</p><p><strong>Greenhouse gases</strong> such as carbon dioxide (CO<sub>2</sub>), methane (CH<sub>4</sub>), water vapour (H<sub>2</sub>O) and nitrous oxide (N<sub>2</sub>O) absorb some of that infrared and send part of it back down. This is the <strong>greenhouse effect</strong>. It is natural, and we need it: without it Earth's average temperature would be about −18 °C instead of about 15 °C.</p><p>The <strong>enhanced greenhouse effect</strong> is the extra warming caused by humans adding more greenhouse gases, mainly by burning fossil fuels and clearing forests. More gas traps more infrared, so more energy stays in the Earth system.</p><div class="admonition tip"><p style='margin:0;'><strong>Natural</strong> greenhouse effect = keeps Earth liveable. <strong>Enhanced</strong> greenhouse effect = human-made extra, causing global warming.</p></div>

## The evidence now

<ul><li><strong>Carbon dioxide:</strong> about 280 ppm (parts per million) before the Industrial Revolution. The monthly mean at Mauna Loa, Hawai‘i, was <strong>426 ppm</strong> in September 2026. That is about 50% higher, and the highest in about 2 million years.</li><li><strong>Global temperature:</strong> 2025 was about <strong>1.43 °C</strong> above pre-industrial (1850–1900) levels. The 11 years from 2015 to 2025 are the 11 warmest on record.</li><li><strong>Australia:</strong> has warmed by about <strong>1.51 °C</strong> since national records began in 1910.</li><li><strong>The ocean:</strong> more than <strong>91%</strong> of the extra heat trapped by greenhouse gases goes into the ocean (hydrosphere). Ocean heat content reached a record in 2025 for the 9th year in a row, and global sea level was about 11 cm higher than in 1993.</li></ul><p>Notice how the spheres connect: gases added to the <strong>atmosphere</strong> trap energy that ends up mostly in the <strong>hydrosphere</strong>, which affects coral reefs and fish in the <strong>biosphere</strong>.</p>

## Guided example

<p>A student says: <em>"The greenhouse effect is bad. We should get rid of it."</em> Use the spheres model to explain what is right and what is wrong about this.</p>
<details class="success"><summary>Show answer</summary>
<p><strong>Wrong part:</strong> the greenhouse effect itself is natural and essential. Greenhouse gases in the atmosphere absorb infrared from the surface and send some back down. Without this Earth would average about −18 °C, and the hydrosphere would be mostly frozen.</p><p><strong>Right part:</strong> the <em>enhanced</em> greenhouse effect is a problem. Burning fossil fuels moves carbon from the geosphere into the atmosphere much faster than plants and the ocean can take it back, so more infrared is trapped and the planet warms.</p><p><strong>Better statement:</strong> "We need the natural greenhouse effect, but we should reduce the extra greenhouse gases humans are adding."</p>
</details>

## Exam skill: Describe a trend using data, then explain it

<div class="admonition note"><p class="admonition-title">What a full answer includes</p><ul><li>the overall direction (increasing, decreasing, no clear change)</li><li>at least two data values with units (usually the first and last)</li><li>the size of the change (subtract) and, if useful, the rate (change ÷ time)</li><li>whether the change is speeding up, slowing down or steady, or any exceptions to the pattern</li><li>an explanation that uses science ideas (cause → effect), kept separate from the description</li></ul></div>

<p><em>Worked example</em></p>
<p><strong>Data:</strong> Mauna Loa CO<sub>2</sub>, 1960–2025 (the graph from this lesson).</p><p><strong>Direction:</strong> CO<sub>2</sub> increased in every five-year step.</p><p><strong>Data with units:</strong> from 316.9 ppm in 1960 to about 425 ppm in 2025.</p><p><strong>Size of change:</strong> about 108 ppm over 65 years.</p><p><strong>Rate:</strong> the increase sped up: about 0.9 ppm per year from 1960 to 1970, but about 2.4 ppm per year from 2010 to 2020.</p><p><strong>Explanation:</strong> humans have burned more fossil fuel each decade, moving carbon from the geosphere into the atmosphere faster than plants and oceans can absorb it.</p>
