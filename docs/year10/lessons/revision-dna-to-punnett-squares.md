## Mutations

<p>A <strong>mutation</strong> is a permanent change in the sequence of bases in DNA.</p><ul><li><strong>Types:</strong> a base can be swapped for another (<strong>substitution</strong>), added (<strong>insertion</strong>) or lost (<strong>deletion</strong>). Bigger mutations change part of a chromosome, or a whole chromosome is extra or missing.</li><li><strong>Causes:</strong> random mistakes when DNA is copied before a cell divides, and <strong>mutagens</strong> such as UV light, X-rays and some chemicals (for example in tobacco smoke).</li><li><strong>Effects:</strong> most are <strong>neutral</strong> (no effect). Some are <strong>harmful</strong> (for example, causing a genetic disorder or cancer). A few are <strong>helpful</strong> (for example, the mutation that lets some adults digest the sugar in milk).</li></ul><div class="admonition tip"><p style='margin:0;'><strong>Inherited or not?</strong> Only a mutation in a <strong>gamete</strong> (sperm or egg), or in the cells that make gametes, can be passed to children. A mutation in a skin cell caused by sunburn can lead to skin cancer, but it is not inherited.</p></div>

## Key terms and the Punnett square method

(only tt is short)</td></tr></table><p><strong>The method, every time:</strong></p><ol><li><strong>Key:</strong> choose a letter. Capital = dominant allele, lower case = recessive allele.</li><li><strong>Parents' genotypes:</strong> write each parent's two alleles. Use clues like "pure-breeding" (homozygous) or "has a parent with the recessive trait" (must carry one recessive allele).</li><li><strong>Gametes:</strong> each parent passes on <em>one</em> allele per gamete (meiosis). Put one parent's alleles across the top and the other's down the side.</li><li><strong>Fill the square,</strong> then count: each box is a 1 in 4 (25%) chance for <em>each</em> child.</li><li><strong>Answer the question asked:</strong> genotype ratio, phenotype ratio or % chance, with words, not just letters.</li></ol><div class="admonition tip"><p style='margin:0;'><strong>Ratio or %?</strong> 3 boxes out of 4 = a ratio of 3:1 = 75%. The % is the <em>chance for each child</em>. It does not mean exactly 3 of every 4 children will have that trait, just as flipping a coin twice does not always give one head.</p></div>

<details class="example"><summary>Worked example: fruit-fly wings</summary>
<p>In fruit flies, normal wings (V) are dominant to short, crumpled vestigial wings (v). Two flies, both heterozygous for normal wings, are crossed. Give the genotype ratio, the phenotype ratio and the % chance that an offspring has vestigial wings.</p><hr><p><strong>1. Key:</strong> V = normal wings (dominant), v = vestigial wings (recessive).</p><p><strong>2. Parents:</strong> both heterozygous, so <strong>Vv × Vv</strong>.</p><p><strong>3. Gametes:</strong> each parent makes V or v gametes.</p><table style='border-collapse:collapse;text-align:center;margin:8px 0;font-size:1.05em;'><tr><td style='padding:6px 14px;'></td><td style='border:1px solid #6b7280;padding:6px 14px;background:#eef2ff;font-weight:bold;'>V</td><td style='border:1px solid #6b7280;padding:6px 14px;background:#eef2ff;font-weight:bold;'>v</td></tr><tr><td style='border:1px solid #6b7280;padding:6px 14px;background:#eef2ff;font-weight:bold;'>V</td><td style='border:1px solid #6b7280;padding:6px 14px;background:#ffffff;'>VV</td><td style='border:1px solid #6b7280;padding:6px 14px;background:#ffffff;'>Vv</td></tr><tr><td style='border:1px solid #6b7280;padding:6px 14px;background:#eef2ff;font-weight:bold;'>v</td><td style='border:1px solid #6b7280;padding:6px 14px;background:#ffffff;'>Vv</td><td style='border:1px solid #6b7280;padding:6px 14px;background:#ffffff;'>vv</td></tr></table><p><strong>4. Count:</strong> VV 1, Vv 2, vv 1.</p><p><strong>5. Answer:</strong> genotype ratio <strong>1 VV : 2 Vv : 1 vv</strong>. Phenotype ratio <strong>3 normal : 1 vestigial</strong>. Only vv has vestigial wings, so the chance is 1 in 4 = <strong>25%</strong>.</p><p><em>Thinking to copy:</em> a vestigial-winged fly needs a v from <strong>both</strong> parents. Both parents have normal wings but are hiding a v.</p>
</details>

## Exam skill: Punnett squares and % chance

<div class="admonition note"><p class="admonition-title">What a full answer includes</p><ul><li>a key: which letter is dominant and which is recessive</li><li>both parents' genotypes, worked out from the clues</li><li>the gametes each parent can make</li><li>a completed Punnett square</li><li>the answer to the exact question asked (genotype ratio, phenotype ratio or % chance), in words as well as letters</li></ul></div>

<p><em>Worked example</em></p>
<p><strong>Worked example.</strong> In pea plants, round seeds (R) are dominant to wrinkled seeds (r). Two plants, both heterozygous for seed shape, are crossed. What % of the seeds are expected to be round?</p><p><strong>Key:</strong> R = round, r = wrinkled. <strong>Parents:</strong> both heterozygous, so Rr × Rr.</p><table style='border-collapse:collapse;text-align:center;margin:8px 0;font-size:1.05em;'><tr><td style='padding:6px 14px;'></td><td style='border:1px solid #6b7280;padding:6px 14px;background:#eef2ff;font-weight:bold;'>R</td><td style='border:1px solid #6b7280;padding:6px 14px;background:#eef2ff;font-weight:bold;'>r</td></tr><tr><td style='border:1px solid #6b7280;padding:6px 14px;background:#eef2ff;font-weight:bold;'>R</td><td style='border:1px solid #6b7280;padding:6px 14px;background:#ffffff;'>RR</td><td style='border:1px solid #6b7280;padding:6px 14px;background:#ffffff;'>Rr</td></tr><tr><td style='border:1px solid #6b7280;padding:6px 14px;background:#eef2ff;font-weight:bold;'>r</td><td style='border:1px solid #6b7280;padding:6px 14px;background:#ffffff;'>Rr</td><td style='border:1px solid #6b7280;padding:6px 14px;background:#ffffff;'>rr</td></tr></table><p><strong>Count:</strong> 1 RR, 2 Rr, 1 rr. Genotype ratio 1 RR : 2 Rr : 1 rr. Phenotype ratio 3 round : 1 wrinkled.</p><p><strong>Answer:</strong> RR and Rr are both round (one R is enough), so 3 out of 4 are round: <strong>75%</strong>. (Wrinkled = 25%.)</p>

Write both parents' genotypes and the possible genotypes of their children.<br><strong>Prompts:</strong> First write the woman's genotype (non-rolling is recessive). Then ask: what must her partner have got from his non-rolling mother? Then draw the square.
