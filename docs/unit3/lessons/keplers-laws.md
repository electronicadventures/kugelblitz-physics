## Key ideas

- **Kepler's 1st law:** planets move in ellipses with the Sun at one focus.
- **2nd law:** a line from the Sun to a planet sweeps out equal areas in equal times, so planets move fastest when closest to the Sun.
- **3rd law:** T² is proportional to r³ for every object orbiting the same central mass.
- Setting gravity equal to the centripetal force (\(GMm/r^2 = mv^2/r\), with \(v = 2\pi r/T\)) gives the 3rd law. The orbiting mass cancels.
- The move from geocentric (Ptolemy) to heliocentric (Copernicus, Kepler) models was driven by better data (Tycho Brahe) and observations (Galileo).

!!! note "Key formulas (QCAA formula book notation)"

    \[
    \frac{T^2}{r^3} = \frac{4\pi^2}{G M}
    \]

    \[
    \frac{T_a^2}{r_a^3} = \frac{T_b^2}{r_b^3}
    \]


??? example "Worked example"
    **Mars orbits at 1.52 times Earth's orbital radius. Find its period in Earth years.**

    1. \(T_M^2 = T_E^2 (r_M/r_E)^3 = 1^2 \times 1.52^3 = 3.51\)
    2. \(T_M = 1.87\) years

!!! warning "Common mistakes"
    - Using the orbiting planet's mass as M.
    - Mixing units (years with metres) within the 4π²/GM form.
