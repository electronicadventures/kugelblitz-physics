# Gravitational fields and field strength

<span class="checklist-codes">G3.3</span> <span class="checklist-codes">G3.4</span>

!!! abstract "By the end of this lesson you should be able to"
    - Describe the concept of gravitational fields. <span class="checklist-codes">G3.3</span>
    - Solve problems involving gravitational field strength at a distance from an object using g = F/m = GM/r^2. <span class="checklist-codes">G3.4</span>

## Key ideas

- A **gravitational field** is the region around a mass where another mass experiences a force.
- Field strength g is the force per kilogram; its units N kg⁻¹ are the same as m s⁻².
- Field lines point towards the mass; closer lines mean a stronger field.
- g depends only on the source mass and the distance from its centre, not on the test mass.

!!! note "Key formulas (QCAA formula book notation)"

    \[
    g = \frac{F}{m} = \frac{G M}{r^2}
    \]


??? example "Worked example"
    **Find g at the surface of Mars (M = 6.42 × 10²³ kg, r = 3.39 × 10⁶ m).**

    1. \(g = \frac{(6.67\times10^{-11})(6.42\times10^{23})}{(3.39\times10^6)^2} = 3.73\) N kg⁻¹

!!! warning "Common mistakes"
    - Using the mass of the object instead of the planet.
    - Drawing field lines pointing away from the mass.
