## Key ideas

- Every mass attracts every other mass. The force is proportional to the product of the masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their **centres**.
- The forces on the two masses are equal and opposite (Newton's third law), whatever the masses.
- Inverse square: double the distance → ¼ the force; triple → 1/9.
- For an object above a planet, r = planet radius + altitude.

!!! note "Key formulas (QCAA formula book notation)"

    \[
    F = \frac{G M m}{r^2}
    \]

    \[
    G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11}\ \text{N m}^2\,\text{kg}^{-2}
    \]


??? example "Worked example"
    **Find the gravitational force between Earth (5.97 × 10²⁴ kg) and a 1000 kg satellite 400 km above the surface (Earth radius 6.37 × 10⁶ m).**

    1. \(r = 6.37 \times 10^6 + 4.0 \times 10^5 = 6.77 \times 10^6\) m
    2. \(F = \frac{(6.67\times10^{-11})(5.97\times10^{24})(1000)}{(6.77\times10^6)^2} = 8.7 \times 10^3\) N

!!! warning "Common mistakes"
    - Using the altitude instead of the centre-to-centre distance.
    - Forgetting to square r.
    - Thinking the smaller mass feels a bigger force.
