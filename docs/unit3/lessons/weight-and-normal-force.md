# Weight, normal force and free-body diagrams

<span class="checklist-codes">G2.1</span> <span class="checklist-codes">G2.2</span>

!!! abstract "By the end of this lesson you should be able to"
    - Solve problems involving force due to gravity (weight) and mass using F_g = mg. <span class="checklist-codes">G2.1</span>
    - Describe the concept of normal force. <span class="checklist-codes">G2.2</span>

## Key ideas

- **Mass** (kg) is the amount of matter and doesn't change with location. **Weight** (N) is the gravitational force on it.
- The **normal force** is the push of a surface on an object, always perpendicular to the surface.
- A **free-body diagram** shows the object as a dot with every force acting on it as an arrow from that dot, labelled and roughly to scale.
- On a flat surface with no other vertical forces, the normal force equals the weight. That's not true on slopes or when pulling at an angle.

!!! note "Key formulas (QCAA formula book notation)"

    \[
    F_g = m g
    \]

    \[
    F_{net} = m a
    \]


??? example "Worked example"
    **A 12 kg box rests on the floor. Find its weight and the normal force.**

    1. \(F_g = 12 \times 9.8 = 118\) N downwards
    2. Balanced vertically, so \(F_N = 118\) N upwards

!!! warning "Common mistakes"
    - Giving weight in kg.
    - Drawing the normal force vertically on a slope.
    - Including forces the object exerts on other things.
