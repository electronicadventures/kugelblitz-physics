## Key ideas

- Gravity provides the centripetal force for an orbit; orbital speed and period depend on the central mass and the radius, not the satellite's mass.
- **Low Earth orbit** (a few hundred km up, period ≈ 90 min): imaging, ISS. **Geostationary** (≈ 36 000 km up, period 24 h, above the equator): stays above one point, used for communications and weather.
- Choosing an orbit trades coverage, resolution, revisit time and launch cost.
- Thousands of satellites and debris fragments are tracked internationally to avoid collisions.

!!! note "Key formulas (QCAA formula book notation)"

    \[
    v = \sqrt{\frac{G M}{r}}
    \]

    \[
    T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{r^3}{G M}}
    \]


??? example "Worked example"
    **Find the orbital radius of a geostationary satellite (T = 86 400 s).**

    1. \(r = \sqrt[3]{\frac{G M T^2}{4\pi^2}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{(6.67\times10^{-11})(5.97\times10^{24})(86400)^2}{4\pi^2}} = 4.22\times10^7\) m

!!! warning "Common mistakes"
    - Giving the altitude when the radius is asked for (or vice versa).
    - Thinking heavier satellites need to orbit faster.
