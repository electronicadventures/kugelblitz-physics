## Key ideas

- Because the velocity's direction changes, there is an acceleration: the **centripetal acceleration**, pointing to the centre.
- A **net inward force** (the centripetal force) is needed. It is not a new force: it is provided by a real force such as tension, friction, gravity or the normal force.
- There is no outward 'centrifugal' force in an inertial frame. The outward feeling is your inertia.
- The force is perpendicular to the velocity, so it changes direction but not speed.

!!! note "Key formulas (QCAA formula book notation)"

    \[
    a_c = \frac{v^2}{r}
    \]

    \[
    F_c = F_{net} = \frac{m v^2}{r}
    \]


??? example "Worked example"
    **A 0.20 kg ball on a 0.80 m string is whirled horizontally at 5.0 m s⁻¹. Find the tension (ignore gravity's effect on the string angle).**

    1. \(F_c = 0.20 \times 5.0^2 / 0.80 = 6.3\) N, provided by the tension

!!! warning "Common mistakes"
    - Drawing a centripetal force *as well as* the real force on a free-body diagram.
    - Forgetting to square v.
    - Pointing the acceleration along the velocity.
