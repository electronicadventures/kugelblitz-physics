## Reading the periodic table

<p>The periodic table lists the elements in order of <strong>atomic number</strong> (the number of protons in the nucleus).</p><ul><li>A <strong>group</strong> is a column. Elements in the same group react in similar ways.</li><li>A <strong>period</strong> is a row.</li></ul><p>Each box shows the atomic number (top), the symbol (middle) and the mass number (bottom).</p>

## Electron shells

<p>Electrons move around the nucleus in <strong>shells</strong> (energy levels). For the first 20 elements, fill the shells from the inside out:</p>
<ul><li>shell 1 holds up to <strong>2</strong> electrons</li><li>shell 2 holds up to <strong>8</strong></li><li>shell 3 holds up to <strong>8</strong></li><li>shell 4 holds the rest (up to 2 for calcium)</li></ul>
<p>The <strong>electron configuration</strong> lists the electrons in each shell, from the inside out: sodium is <strong>2, 8, 1</strong>. A neutral atom has the same number of electrons as protons, so the atomic number tells you how many electrons to place.</p>
<p>The electrons in the outer shell are the <strong>valence electrons</strong>. They decide how an atom reacts.</p>

### Try it: build the shells

<p>Pick an element, then add electrons one at a time. Watch which shell each one goes into.</p>

<p>Draw the electron shells of aluminium and write its electron configuration.</p><hr>

## Predicting reactivity

<p>Atoms are most <strong>stable</strong> with a <strong>full outer shell</strong>. Atoms react by losing, gaining or sharing electrons to get one.</p>
<ul><li><strong>Group 1 (alkali metals):</strong> 1 valence electron, lost easily → <strong>very reactive</strong> metals. Reactivity <strong>increases</strong> down the group (the outer electron is further from the nucleus, so it is lost more easily).</li>
<li><strong>Group 17 (halogens):</strong> 7 valence electrons, need just 1 more → <strong>very reactive</strong> non-metals. Reactivity <strong>decreases</strong> down the group (fluorine is the most reactive).</li>
<li><strong>Group 18 (noble gases):</strong> outer shell already full → <strong>unreactive</strong>.</li>
<li>Groups in between (2, 13–16) are less reactive than Groups 1 and 17 in the same period: they have more electrons to lose or gain.</li></ul>
<p><strong>Bonus link (ionic bonding):</strong> when a Group 1 metal meets a Group 17 non-metal, the metal atom gives its 1 valence electron to the non-metal atom. Both end up with full outer shells, now as charged <strong>ions</strong>, which attract each other. Potassium and chlorine make potassium chloride this way.</p>

<div class="full-answer"><p><strong>What a full answer includes: "Predict the reactivity … justify"</strong></p>
<ol><li><strong>Prediction:</strong> very reactive, reactive, or unreactive (and compare the elements if there is more than one).</li>
<li><strong>Position:</strong> its group and period, and the family name (alkali metal, halogen, noble gas).</li>
<li><strong>Configuration:</strong> e.g. 2, 8, 8, 1.</li>
<li><strong>Valence electrons:</strong> how many, and whether the atom loses, gains or keeps them to reach a full outer shell.</li></ol></div>

<details class="example" id="worked-example-predicting-reactivity"><summary>Worked example: predicting the reactivity of potassium, chlorine and argon</summary>
<p>Predict the reactivity of potassium, chlorine and argon. Justify each prediction.</p><hr>
<table><thead><tr><th>Potassium (K)</th><th>Chlorine (Cl)</th><th>Argon (Ar)</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr>
<td><strong>Very reactive</strong> (the most reactive of the three)<ul><li>Group 1, Period 4: an alkali metal</li><li>Configuration 2, 8, 8, 1</li><li>1 valence electron, lost easily to leave a full outer shell</li><li>4 shells: the outer electron is far from the nucleus, so potassium is more reactive than lithium or sodium</li></ul></td>
<td><strong>Very reactive</strong><ul><li>Group 17, Period 3: a halogen (non-metal)</li><li>Configuration 2, 8, 7</li><li>7 valence electrons: gains 1 electron to fill its outer shell</li><li>Less reactive than fluorine, which is higher in Group 17</li></ul></td>
<td><strong>Unreactive</strong><ul><li>Group 18, Period 3: a noble gas</li><li>Configuration 2, 8, 8</li><li>8 valence electrons: outer shell already full</li><li>No need to lose or gain electrons, so it does not usually form compounds</li></ul></td>
</tr></tbody></table>
